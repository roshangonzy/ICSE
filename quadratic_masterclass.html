Quadratic Equations

Master the nature of roots, factorisation techniques, and word problems. This module is heavily weighted in the ICSE Grade 10 Board Examinations.

The Standard Form

A quadratic equation is a second-degree polynomial equation. The standard form is always written as:

ax² + bx + c = 0
  • a is the coefficient of x² (and a ≠ 0)
  • b is the coefficient of x
  • c is the constant term

Solving by Factorisation

The most common method is "Splitting the Middle Term". The goal is to find two numbers that multiply to (a × c) and add up to b.

ICSE Step-Marking Tip

Always state the Zero Product Rule explicitly if solving a subjective question. If (x - p)(x - q) = 0, you must write:

"Either x - p = 0 OR x - q = 0"
Skipping this line can cost you a step mark!

The Quadratic Formula

When an equation cannot be factorised easily, or when the question asks for the answer "correct to two decimal places", use Sridharacharya's formula:

x = [ -b ± √(b² - 4ac) ] / 2a

Common Trap

Forgetting to divide the entire numerator by 2a. Many students only divide the square root part!

The Fix

Draw a long fraction bar across the whole expression before writing 2a underneath.

Nature of Roots (Discriminant)

The value inside the square root, b² - 4ac, is called the Discriminant (Δ). It tells you what kind of roots the equation has without actually solving it.

Δ > 0 Roots are Real & Distinct (Unequal)
Δ = 0 Roots are Real & Equal (Coincident)
Δ < 0 Roots are Imaginary (No Real Roots)
