Quadratic Equations Traps

A curated list of strict ICSE step-marking requirements, examiner comments, and the most common pitfalls that cost students full marks.

The "Missing Root" Trap

High Frequency

The Mistake

Taking the square root of both sides but forgetting the ± sign.
x² = 16 ➔ x = 4

The Fix

Quadratic equations must have two roots. Always write the ± sign immediately.
x = ±√16 ➔ x = 4, x = -4

Decimal Instruction Trap

Critical

The Mistake

Leaving the final answer in surd form (e.g., √13) or fraction form when the question specifically states "Correct to 2 decimal places".

The Fix

Use the Quadratic Formula. Calculate to three decimal places, then properly round off to two.
2.456 ➔ 2.46

Zero Product Rule Skipped

Step-Mark Loss

The Mistake

Jumping directly from factorisation to the final roots without showing the logical deduction.
(x-3)(x+2) = 0 ➔ x = 3, -2

The Fix

ICSE strictly requires you to state:
Either (x-3) = 0 OR (x+2) = 0
This line carries a dedicated step mark!

Discriminant Sign Error

Calculation

The Mistake

Mishandling double negative signs when calculating b² - 4ac, especially when 'c' is negative.
4 - 4(1)(-3) ➔ 4 - 12 = -8

The Fix

Use brackets religiously to separate the operation from the negative value.
4 - [4 × 1 × (-3)] ➔ 4 - [-12] ➔ 4 + 12 = 16
