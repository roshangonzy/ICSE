1 Translational vs. Rotational Motion

When a force acts on a rigid body, the resulting motion depends entirely on whether the body is free to move or pivoted at a specific point.

Translational Motion

When a force acts on a free, stationary rigid body, the body moves in a straight line in the direction of the applied force. Every particle of the body moves with the same velocity.

Example: Pushing a book across a flat table.

Rotational Motion

If the body is pivoted (hinged) at a point, the applied force does not move the body linearly. Instead, it turns or rotates the body about the fixed point or axis of rotation.

Example: Pushing a door to open it.

2 Moment of Force (Torque)

The turning effect of a force acting on a body about an axis is called the Moment of Force (or Torque).

Mathematical Formulation

Torque is equal to the product of the magnitude of the force and the perpendicular distance of the line of action of the force from the axis of rotation.

$$ \tau = F \times d $$

Crucial Unit Conversions

The SI unit of moment of force is Newton-metre ($N\cdot m$). The CGS unit is dyne-cm. In gravitational units, it is measured in $kgf\cdot m$ or $gf\cdot cm$.

$1 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = 10^7 \text{ dyne}\cdot\text{cm}$
$1 \text{ kgf}\cdot\text{m} = 9.8 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$
$1 \text{ gf}\cdot\text{cm} = 980 \text{ dyne}\cdot\text{cm}$

ICSE Deadly Trap: Work vs. Torque

Both Work and Torque have the formula $Force \times Distance$, but they are fundamentally different! Work is a scalar quantity (Unit: Joule). Torque is a vector quantity (Unit: $N\cdot m$).

NEVER write Joule as the unit for Moment of Force. The ICSE examiner will instantly give you a 0 for that part.

Sign Convention for Vector Direction

  • If the force turns the body in an Anticlockwise direction, the moment is taken as Positive ($+$).
  • If the force turns the body in a Clockwise direction, the moment is taken as Negative ($-$).

3 Couple & Its Moment

In practice, a single force rarely causes pure rotation. Turning a steering wheel or opening a water tap requires two hands applying force in opposite directions.

Formal Definition:

Two equal, opposite, and parallel forces not acting along the same line, form a Couple. A couple is always required to produce pure rotation.

Moment of a Couple

The moment of a couple is equal to the product of either force and the perpendicular distance between the lines of action of both forces (called the couple arm).

$$ \text{Moment of Couple} = F \times d $$

4 Equilibrium & Principle of Moments

When a number of forces act on a body, and it does not change its state of rest or of uniform motion, the body is said to be in equilibrium.

1. Static Equilibrium

When a body remains in a state of rest under the influence of several forces.
Example: A book resting on a table (Weight downwards is balanced by Normal Reaction upwards).

2. Dynamic Equilibrium

When a body remains in a state of uniform motion under the influence of several forces.
Example: A raindrop falling with a constant terminal velocity.

Core Law

The Principle of Moments

According to the principle of moments, if a body is in rotational equilibrium, the sum of the anticlockwise moments about any point is equal to the sum of the clockwise moments about that point.

$$ \sum \text{Anticlockwise Moments} = \sum \text{Clockwise Moments} $$

Conditions for Equilibrium:

  • The resultant of all forces acting on the body must be zero.
  • The algebraic sum of moments of all forces about the point of rotation must be zero.

5 Centre of Gravity (CG)

A body can be considered to be made up of thousands of tiny particles. The earth pulls every particle downwards. The Centre of Gravity is the specific point about which the algebraic sum of moments of weights of all the particles constituting the body is zero. The entire weight of the body is considered to act at this single point.

Can the CG lie outside the material of the body?

Yes! The CG of a uniform ring (like a bangle or a boomerang) lies at its geometric center, which is entirely in the empty space outside the solid mass.

6 Uniform Circular Motion

When a particle moves with a constant speed in a circular path, its motion is called uniform circular motion.

The Speed vs. Velocity Distinction

In uniform circular motion, the speed is strictly constant. However, because the particle is continuously changing its direction at every point on the circle, its velocity is continuously changing.

Since velocity changes, uniform circular motion is always an accelerated motion. The direction of velocity at any point is along the tangent to the circle at that point.

7 Centripetal & Centrifugal Forces

Centripetal Force

A real force acting on a body moving in a circular path, in a direction towards the centre of the circular path.

Example: Tension in the string when whirling a stone; Gravitational force of the sun on planets.

Centrifugal Force

A fictitious (pseudo) force assumed by an observer moving with the body to explain their outward push. It acts radially outwards.

It is NOT a real force. It is the result of inertia.

Action & Reaction Myth

Students often state "Centrifugal force is the reaction force to Centripetal force". This is completely wrong. Action and reaction forces act on different bodies. Both centripetal and centrifugal forces act on the same body. The reaction to the tension pulling the stone inwards (centripetal) is the stone pulling the hand outwards.

Final Exam Trap Checklist

The "Perpendicular" Keyword

When defining Moment of Force, writing "Force $\times$ distance" gets 0 marks. You MUST write "Force $\times$ perpendicular distance of the line of action from the pivot".

Metre Rule Numericals

Always remember that a "uniform metre rule" has its own weight acting exactly at the 50 cm mark. Students constantly forget to include the weight of the scale itself when calculating moments in equilibrium numericals.

Solid vs. Hollow Cone

Memorize the table: Solid Cone CG = $h/4$. Hollow Cone CG = $h/3$. This is a guaranteed 1-mark objective question.
